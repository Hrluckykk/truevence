BGV Correction · Step-by-Step

How to correct an incorrect BGV report.

An incorrect background verification report can cost you a job offer. But you have the right to correct it — and a clear process to follow. Here is the step-by-step guide to fixing wrong BGV findings in India, including the documents you need, the timelines to expect, and what to do if the employer or vendor does not respond.

July 2026 10 min read Truevence Editorial Team Filed under Candidate Rights
§ 01 — Why corrections happen

BGV reports are wrong more often than you think.

Background verification is not infallible. It relies on databases, human data entry, and source responses — all of which can introduce errors. In India, where much of the verification infrastructure is still maturing, incorrect findings are common.

The most frequent causes of incorrect BGV reports include name mismatches in government databases, outdated university records, EPFO data gaps, court records attributed to the wrong person, and simple data-entry errors by the verification agency.

Common cause Name mismatch — middle name missing, spelling variation, maiden vs married name.
Common cause Outdated source data — degree awarded but university database not updated; case closed but court record shows pending.
Common cause Wrong person's record — same-name confusion in criminal or court database searches.
Common cause Verifier process error — wrong source contacted, typo in ID number, no follow-up when source did not respond.

An incorrect report is not a dead end. It is a data problem with a defined correction process.

§ 02 — What you can correct

What can be corrected.

Not every finding can be corrected. You can correct errors of fact and data. You cannot correct an accurate adverse finding simply because you do not want it on your report.

  • Personal data errors. Wrong name spelling, incorrect date of birth, wrong address, mismatched ID number, incorrect father's name. These are data errors and must be corrected.
  • Misattributed records. A criminal or court record that belongs to another person with a similar name. A case that was withdrawn, acquitted, or quashed but shows as active.
  • Outdated findings. A degree that was awarded but the university database was not updated. An employment period that is correct but the EPFO record is incomplete.
  • Verification process errors. The verifier contacted the wrong institution, queried the wrong jurisdiction, or mistyped a reference number.
  • Incorrect classification. A check marked as "Discrepancy" when it should be "Unable to Verify" because the source simply did not respond.

What you cannot correct: a genuine criminal conviction, a degree you did not complete, an employment period you falsified, or any adverse finding that is accurate. The correction process fixes errors, not facts you dislike.

§ 03 — Step-by-step

The correction process, step by step.

Here is the exact sequence to follow when you need to correct a BGV report.

Step Action Timeline
1. Get the report Request a full copy of your BGV report from HR or the vendor Immediately
2. Identify the error Pinpoint exactly which check is wrong, what it says, and what it should say 1-2 days
3. Gather evidence Collect certificates, letters, ID proofs, court orders that prove the correct information 2-3 days
4. Submit correction request Write a formal request with the error, the correction, and evidence attached Within dispute window (7-15 days)
5. Acknowledge receipt Confirm the employer/vendor has received your request 3-5 business days
6. Follow up If no response, follow up in writing and escalate to the grievance officer 7-10 days
7. Re-verification If accepted, the check is re-run with corrected data or the source is re-contacted 7-15 days
8. Corrected report Receive the updated report and confirm the error is fixed Upon completion
§ 04 — Documents you need

What documents to attach.

The strength of your correction request depends on the evidence you attach. Here is what to gather for each type of error.

1.

Name or identity error.

Attach your Aadhaar, PAN, passport, or any government ID that shows the correct spelling and details. If there was a name change (marriage, legal), attach the supporting document. The ID must match what the verifier should have used.

Aadhaar PAN Passport
2.

Education error.

Attach your degree certificate, marksheets, and if available, a verification letter from the university or a NAD (National Academic Depository) record. If the university database is outdated, the certificate itself is your evidence.

Degree certificate Marksheets NAD record
3.

Employment error.

Attach your offer letter, appointment letter, relieving letter, experience letter, and payslips for the disputed period. If EPFO data is incomplete, your payslips and Form 16 are the strongest proof. Bank statements showing salary credits also help.

Offer letter Relieving letter Payslips / Form 16
4.

Criminal or court record error.

Attach the court order showing the case was withdrawn, acquitted, quashed, or that you were not the person named. If the record belongs to someone else, attach proof of your identity and any document showing the other person's details. This is the most serious type of error and needs the strongest evidence.

Court order Acquittal proof Identity proof
5.

Address error.

Attach your rental agreement, utility bill, bank statement, or Aadhaar showing the correct address. If the address changed recently, attach both old and new proofs with a clear timeline. Utility bills and rental agreements are the most accepted.

Rental agreement Utility bill Bank statement
§ 05 — Sample request

What a correction request should look like.

Your correction request should be clear, factual, and evidence-backed. Here is a template you can adapt.

Template · Correction request

A clear, evidence-backed request gets faster resolution.

Subject: Correction request for BGV report — [Your Name] — [Candidate ID / Application Reference]

Dear [HR / Grievance Officer],

I am writing to request a correction to my background verification report dated [date]. The report contains the following error:

Check affected: [Education / Employment / Criminal / Address / Identity]

What the report says: [Quote the incorrect finding]

What is correct: [State the correct information]

Evidence attached: [List the documents you are attaching]

I request that this check be re-verified with the corrected information and that the report be updated accordingly. I am available to provide any further documentation required.

Please acknowledge this request within 3-5 business days and confirm the expected timeline for resolution.

Regards,
[Your Name]
[Contact details]

Keep a copy of this request and all attachments. If you do not receive a response, follow up in writing and escalate.

§ 06 — If they don't respond

What to do if the employer or vendor ignores your request.

Under the DPDP Act, data fiduciaries must provide a grievance redressal mechanism and respond to correction requests. If they do not, you have escalation options.

  • Follow up in writing. Send a second email referencing your original request and the date it was sent. Keep it factual.
  • Escalate to the grievance officer. Under the DPDP Act, every data fiduciary must designate a grievance officer. Ask for that person's contact details and write to them directly.
  • Contact the BGV vendor directly. If the employer is not responsive, reach out to the verification agency that produced the report. They have their own grievance process.
  • Document everything. Keep records of every email, every response (or lack of one), and every document you submitted. This is your evidence trail.
  • Escalate to the Data Protection Board. If the fiduciary does not resolve your grievance, you can escalate to the Data Protection Board of India. This is a formal complaint under the DPDP Act.
  • Seek legal advice if needed. If the incorrect report causes you to lose a job offer or suffer material harm, consult an employment lawyer about your options.
