Why Background Verification Is Important — don't let one bad hire cost you everything.

One bad hire can cost a company more than just money — it can destroy team morale, damage your reputation, and even lead to legal trouble. Here's why background verification should be a non-negotiable step in your hiring process.

July 2026 7 min read Truevence Editorial Team Filed under Hiring Risk
§ 01 — The real cost

The real cost of a bad hire.

According to a study by the Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM), the cost of a bad hire can be up to 5 times the employee's annual salary. For a mid-level manager earning ₹12 lakh per annum, that's a potential loss of ₹60 lakh — not including the damage to client relationships and team morale.

In India, where CV fraud rates are alarmingly high, the risk is even greater. Without proper background checks, you could be hiring someone who has:

  • Falsified their educational qualifications
  • Hidden a criminal record
  • Exaggerated their previous job title or salary
  • Worked for a competitor under a different name
The risk A bad hire can cost up to 5× annual salary — plus morale and reputation damage.
The cause CV fraud rates in India are alarmingly high, and manual checks miss most of it.
The fix A structured BGV process catches fraud before it walks through your door.

The cost of a background check is a rounding error. The cost of a bad hire is a line item that can kill a quarter.

§ 03 — Real-world examples

What happens when you skip BGV.

Two cases. Two preventable disasters.

A simple background check could have saved crores and reputations.

Case 1: The Fake IIT Graduate. A Gurgaon-based startup hired a "senior engineer" claiming an IIT Delhi degree. Six months later, a client audit revealed the degree was fake. The startup lost the client contract worth ₹45 lakh and faced a lawsuit for misrepresentation.

Case 2: The Undisclosed Criminal Record. A BFSI company in Noida onboarded a relationship manager without a criminal check. The employee was later arrested for a previous fraud case, causing panic among customers and regulatory scrutiny. A simple background check could have prevented the hire.

Both cases share the same root cause: no structured BGV process.
