Why AML and Sanction Screening is a Legal Imperative

Under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) and guidelines from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), banks, NBFCs, insurance companies, and fintech firms must verify that customers, employees, and vendors are not involved in money laundering or terrorist financing. Failure to do so can result in heavy fines, licence revocation, and criminal liability for the management. Even outside the BFSI sector, hiring a senior executive or finance professional with hidden sanctions exposure can freeze your company's bank accounts and destroy business relationships overnight.

Common Questions About AML and Sanction Screening

Is AML screening mandatory for all companies?

It is mandatory for banks, NBFCs, payment providers, and any entity regulated by RBI, SEBI, or IRDAI. Even if you are not regulated, if your business involves international transactions or high-risk clients, voluntary AML screening is a wise risk management practice.

How often should AML screening be refreshed?

RBI guidelines suggest periodic updating of KYC and AML records. For employees, an annual refresh is common. For customers, it may be every 2-5 years depending on risk categorisation. Truevence can schedule automated periodic checks.

What happens if a candidate matches a sanctions list?

We provide you with the details of the match, the source list, and the matching identifiers. You should then conduct enhanced due diligence. In many cases, hiring a designated individual is prohibited by law. Our report gives you the facts to decide in consultation with your legal team.

Can AML screening replace a criminal background check?

No. AML screening focuses on financial crime watchlists and international restrictions. It does not cover local criminal convictions for theft, assault, or other non-financial offences. For a complete background check, combine AML screening with a criminal court check.
