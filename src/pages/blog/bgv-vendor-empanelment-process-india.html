BGV vendor empanelment process in India: a complete guide.

Empanelment is the process that turns vendor selection from a one-off event into a governed capability. It gives your organisation an approved panel of background verification vendors that can be awarded work without re-running procurement each time. This guide explains the 7-stage empanelment process, the documents required, and the governance that keeps the panel effective over time.

July 2026 10 min read Truevence Editorial Team Filed under Enterprise Procurement
§ 01 — What empanelment is

What BGV vendor empanelment actually is.

Empanelment is the formal process by which an enterprise evaluates, approves, and adds a background verification vendor to its list of approved service providers. Once on the panel, the vendor can be awarded work for a defined period, usually two or three years, without re-running the full procurement cycle for each engagement.

For the organisation, empanelment reduces procurement overhead, increases competition among vendors, and creates a documented record that shows the selection was fair and governed. For the vendor, it provides a predictable commercial relationship and makes them eligible for work without bidding every time. Both sides benefit from the clarity that a formal panel brings.

Purpose Create an approved panel of BGV vendors for multi-year, governed use.
Typical panel size Two to four vendors. Enough for competition, small enough to manage.
Panel validity Two or three years, with an annual review for ongoing compliance.
Led by Procurement, with HR, Legal, Information Security, and Compliance as evaluators.
End-to-end timeline 6 to 10 weeks from RFP issuance to final panel approval.

Empanelment is not a bureaucratic exercise. It is the difference between buying BGV repeatedly and owning a BGV capability.

§ 02 — The 7 stages

The 7-stage empanelment process.

A properly run empanelment follows seven stages. Each stage has clear entry and exit criteria, and each produces a documented output. Skipping any stage creates a gap that typically shows up during an audit or a dispute.

Stage What happens Output
01 Need assessment Define scope, volumes, geographic coverage, and panel size target Empanelment charter
02 RFP issuance Send a structured RFP to a long list of 8 to 12 vendors Issued RFP and vendor acknowledgements
03 Compliance screening Verify mandatory documents from every respondent Compliant vendor shortlist
04 Technical and commercial evaluation Score capability, pricing, turnaround, and technology Ranked evaluation matrix
05 Due diligence and reference checks Call references, verify claims, review financials Due diligence report
06 Approval and agreement Panel approval by committee, agreements signed with each vendor Signed panel agreements
07 Ongoing governance Quarterly reviews, annual re-validation, and panel refresh Panel performance scorecard
§ 03 — Documents

Documents required for BGV vendor empanelment.

The documentation stage is where most empanelment processes either move quickly or stall. Vendors that arrive prepared with all documents ready move through in a week. Vendors that arrive piecemeal can take a month to clear this stage alone. As the buyer, your job is to request everything up front and verify each item independently.

  • Certificate of incorporation with CIN, verifiable on the MCA portal
  • GST registration with GSTIN and active status confirmed independently
  • PAN and TAN matching the entity name used in the contract
  • UIDAI AUA or ASA authorisation if the vendor runs Aadhaar verification
  • ISO 27001 certificate verified on the issuing body's public register
  • SOC 2 Type II report if the vendor handles data through an API or portal
  • Professional indemnity insurance certificate with coverage amounts and expiry
  • Cyber liability insurance with explicit third-party data breach cover
  • Data processing agreement aligned with DPDP Act requirements
  • DPDP compliance documentation including consent framework and retention policy
  • Code of conduct and anti-bribery declaration signed by an authorised signatory
  • Financial statements for the last two financial years, audited where available
  • Sample verification report redacted appropriately, showing the actual output format
  • Client references with contact details for at least two enterprise clients
§ 04 — Evaluation

How to score vendors for empanelment.

An empanelment evaluation should weight capability and compliance heavily, and price moderately. Once empanelled, price can be renegotiated. Capability and compliance gaps are much harder to fix mid-contract.

Weight what actually matters

Compliance and capability should carry more than half the weight.

Compliance and legal (25 percent). DPDP readiness, UIDAI authorisation, insurance, data processing agreement, breach notification timelines. This is the highest-weighted category for a reason. Under the DPDP Act, a compliance failure by the vendor becomes your failure as data fiduciary.

Coverage and capability (20 percent). Which checks the vendor can run, in which cities, at what depth. Specifically: whether they have direct university relationships, whether their EPFO access is direct, and whether their field agent network covers the locations you actually hire from.

Accuracy and quality (15 percent). Documented accuracy rate, sample report quality, quality review process, dispute resolution approach. Ask for their accuracy number and how they measure it.

Turnaround and SLAs (15 percent). Documented per-check turnaround, peak load commitments, SLA remedies, escalation matrix. A turnaround commitment without a remedy is not a commitment.

Pricing and commercials (15 percent). Per-check pricing at your volume band, bulk discount structure, rate lock period, and hidden cost transparency. Look for total cost of ownership, not headline rate.

Technology and integration (5 percent). API availability, ATS integrations, sandbox access, uptime commitments. Weighted lower because it is easiest to work around if the other categories are strong.

References and stability (5 percent). Reference calls completed, financial stability, years in operation, leadership continuity. Used as a tiebreaker between closely-ranked vendors.

§ 05 — Panel structure

How many vendors and how to split work.

Panel size and work allocation are the two decisions that determine whether empanelment actually delivers value or just adds administration.

Decision Recommended approach Reasoning
Panel size Two to four vendors One vendor is concentration risk, more than four dilutes volume and leverage
Primary and backup One primary, one or two secondaries Primary carries 70 to 80 percent of volume, secondaries carry the rest
Work allocation By check type or by geography Some vendors are stronger on identity, others on field verification
Rotation policy Review allocation annually Keeps vendors competitive, allows rebalancing based on performance
New vendor entry Allow mid-cycle additions with committee approval Keeps the panel responsive to business needs without a full re-empanelment
§ 06 — Governance

Governing the panel after empanelment.

Empanelment is not a one-time event. Without ongoing governance, the panel degrades over time: vendors stop performing, new vendors never get a chance, and the original evaluation becomes meaningless. A light governance cadence keeps the panel healthy.

  • Monthly operational review with each active vendor to track turnaround, accuracy, and open issues
  • Quarterly business review with panel leadership for performance data and pipeline discussion
  • Annual compliance re-validation to confirm that certifications, insurance, and UIDAI authorisation are still valid
  • Annual performance scorecard rating each vendor on turnaround, accuracy, escalation handling, and commercial behaviour
  • Panel refresh cycle every two to three years, with a full re-empanelment or targeted additions as needed
  • Escalation path for removing a vendor from the panel mid-cycle if performance falls materially below threshold
    • § 07 — Common mistakes

    Common BGV empanelment mistakes.

    These are the mistakes we see most often in enterprise empanelment processes. Each one is avoidable with modest process discipline.

    • Collecting documents without verifying them. An ISO certificate on the vendor's letterhead is not the same as an ISO certificate that verifies on the issuing body's register.
    • Not checking UIDAI authorisation. Vendors who claim to run Aadhaar checks without AUA or ASA status are operating illegally and exposing you to regulatory action.
    • Weighting price too heavily. The cheapest empanelled vendor often ends up costing more in re-verification, escalations, and compliance risk than the next one up.
    • Empanelling only one vendor. Single-vendor panels create concentration risk. If the vendor has an outage or a quality issue, you have no fallback.
    • Skipping the reference call. Reference customers who are willing to speak honestly about how the vendor behaves under pressure are the single most useful signal.
    • No governance after empanelment. A panel without reviews is just a list. It stops reflecting the reality of vendor performance within a year.
    • Not defining panel removal criteria. Without a removal path, a poorly performing vendor stays on the panel for the full validity period.
