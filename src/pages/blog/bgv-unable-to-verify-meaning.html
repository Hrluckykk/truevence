What does BGV "Unable to Verify" actually mean?

You checked your BGV status and it says "Unable to Verify." It sounds alarming — but in most cases, it is not a fail and not a discrepancy. It means the verifier could not confirm the information with the source, usually because of a data mismatch or a slow institution. Here is what it means, why it happens, and what to do next.

July 2026 8 min read Truevence Editorial Team Filed under BGV Status
§ 01 — The meaning

"Unable to Verify" is not a fail.

When a background verification report shows "Unable to Verify" against a check, it means the verification agency could not independently confirm the information you or the candidate provided. The verifier tried, but the source — a university, a previous employer, a court database — did not return a confirmable result.

This is different from a "Discrepancy" (where the information you provided does not match what the source says) and different from a "Fail" (where the check definitively shows the information is false or the record exists). "Unable to Verify" sits in the middle: the verifier could not confirm or deny.

What it means The verifier could not confirm the information with the source. Not confirmed false. Not confirmed true.
What it is not It is not a discrepancy (mismatch) and not a fail (confirmed false). It is an incomplete check.
Common outcome Most "Unable to Verify" results are resolved with additional documents or a corrected input.
Employer action Employers should not auto-reject on this status. They should request clarification and re-run the check.

"Unable to Verify" means the check is incomplete, not that the candidate lied. Treat it as a request for more information, not a rejection.

§ 02 — Why it happens

Why "Unable to Verify" shows up.

There are a handful of common reasons a check lands on "Unable to Verify." Most have nothing to do with the candidate being dishonest.

1.

Data mismatch.

The name, date of birth, or ID number you submitted does not exactly match the source record. A middle name missing, a maiden name vs married name, a date format difference — any of these can cause the source to return "no match found" rather than a clear result. This is the single most common cause.

Name mismatch DOB format ID number error
2.

Source not responding.

The university registrar, previous employer, or court office did not respond within the verification window. Small institutions, government offices, and former employers with no HR department often simply do not reply. This is a timing problem, not a candidate problem.

University slow Ex-employer silent Court backlog
3.

Database coverage gap.

The source record is not in the digital database the verifier can access. For example, a degree from a university that is not integrated with the National Academic Depository, or employment with a company that never registered for EPFO. Not in the database does not mean not true.

Not digitised Not on NAD No EPFO record
4.

Insufficient documents.

The verifier needed a document that was not submitted — a degree certificate, a relieving letter, a payslip, or an address proof — and could not proceed without it. The check is paused, not failed.

Missing certificate No relieving letter Address proof absent
5.

Technical or process error.

An API timeout, a mistyped input, or a wrong source routed for the check. This is the rarest cause, but it happens. Re-running the check usually resolves it.

API timeout Wrong source Typo in input
§ 03 — How it compares

"Unable to Verify" vs other BGV statuses.

BGV reports use several status labels, and they mean very different things. Here is how "Unable to Verify" sits alongside the others.

Status What it means Severity
Unable to Verify Verifier could not confirm with source Low — request more info
Insufficient Not enough documents to run the check Low — submit documents
Discrepancy Provided info does not match source Medium — needs explanation
On Hold Check paused, awaiting input or source Low — waiting
Failed Confirmed false or adverse record found High — rejection risk
Verified Source confirmed the information None — clear

The key distinction: "Unable to Verify" and "Insufficient" are process states, not verdicts. "Discrepancy" and "Failed" are findings. Employers should treat them very differently.

§ 04 — For candidates

What to do if your status says "Unable to Verify."

If you are a candidate and your BGV status shows "Unable to Verify," do not panic. Here is the sequence to follow.

  • Do not assume it is a rejection. In most cases it is not. It is a request for more information or a signal that the verifier needs another attempt.
  • Identify which check is affected. Look at the report to see whether it is education, employment, address, or another check. The fix depends on which one.
  • Gather the supporting documents. If it is education, get your degree certificate and marksheets. If it is employment, get your relieving letter, offer letter, and payslips.
  • Respond promptly to the verifier. The faster you provide the missing documents or correct the mismatch, the faster the check resolves.
  • Correct any data errors. If your name or date of birth was entered incorrectly, flag it immediately so the check can be re-run.
  • Ask for a re-verification. You are entitled to request that the check be re-run with corrected or supplemented information.
§ 05 — For employers

What employers should do when a check can't be verified.

Employers have a responsibility to handle "Unable to Verify" fairly. Auto-rejecting on this status is both bad practice and, increasingly, a compliance risk.

Fair process · Re-verification · Documentation

Treat "Unable to Verify" as an open question, not a closed door.

Do not auto-reject. "Unable to Verify" is not a finding of dishonesty. Rejecting on this status alone can expose you to claims of unfair hiring practice, especially if the cause was a data mismatch or a slow institution.

Request clarification. Give the candidate a clear, specific request: which check, what document or correction is needed, and a deadline. Document the request and the response.

Re-run the check. Once the candidate supplies the missing document or corrects the data, re-run the check. In the majority of cases, the second run returns "Verified."

Escalate to manual verification if needed. If the digital source cannot confirm, a field agent or manual outreach to the institution may resolve it. This is slower but often succeeds where automated checks fail.

Document the outcome. Whatever the final status, record what was requested, what was provided, and what the final result was. This protects you if the hiring decision is later challenged.

Never let it stall indefinitely. Set a reasonable deadline. If the candidate does not respond and the check cannot be completed, make a documented decision — but make it a conscious one, not an accidental one.

§ 06 — When to worry

When "Unable to Verify" becomes a red flag.

Most "Unable to Verify" results are benign. But there are specific patterns that warrant closer scrutiny.

  • Multiple checks failing on the same document. If education and employment both cannot be verified, and both rely on the same institution or employer, that is worth investigating.
  • Candidate cannot produce any supporting document. If a candidate claims a degree but cannot produce a certificate, marksheet, or verifiable registration number, that is a concern.
  • Source says "no record" rather than "not responding." A silent source is one thing. A source that affirmatively says no record exists is a different matter — that leans toward a discrepancy.
  • Pattern of repeated "Unable to Verify" across multiple employers. One is normal. A pattern across several past roles suggests a deeper issue.
  • Candidate is evasive or slow to respond. A genuine data mismatch is usually resolved quickly. Persistent non-response is itself a signal.

In these cases, escalate from "re-run the check" to "request an explanation and supporting evidence." If the explanation is satisfactory and documents check out, proceed. If not, treat it as a discrepancy.
