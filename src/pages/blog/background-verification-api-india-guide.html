Background verification API in India: the complete guide.

Manual background verification takes 7 to 15 days per candidate and costs thousands of rupees. API-driven verification completes the same checks in under a minute at a fraction of the cost. This guide explains how BGV APIs work, what can be automated, the legal framework under the DPDP Act, and how to choose a provider.

July 2026 10 min read Truevence Editorial Team Filed under BGV Automation
§ 01 — What it is

What is a background verification API?

A background verification API is a programmatic interface that lets your hiring system send candidate details to a verification provider and receive results back automatically. Instead of emailing documents to a vendor and waiting days for a report, your applicant tracking system (ATS) or HR platform calls the API directly and gets a structured response in seconds or minutes.

The shift from manual to API-driven BGV is the same shift that happened with payments, KYC, and identity verification. What used to require back-and-forth communication and human review is now a single API call that returns a machine-readable result your system can act on immediately.

The problem Manual BGV takes 7 to 15 days per candidate and costs ₹800 to ₹5,000, depending on the checks.
The API fix Automated checks complete in under 60 seconds at ₹50 to ₹800 per candidate, depending on the verification type.
Who benefits Staffing firms hiring at volume, gig platforms onboarding daily, and any company with more than a handful of hires per month.
What stays manual Physical address visits and some university verification still require field agents. APIs handle the digital checks.

An API does not replace human judgment. It removes the waiting and the paperwork so your team can focus on the judgment part.

§ 02 — What can be automated

Which BGV checks can be automated in India.

Not every check is equally automatable. Here is the practical breakdown, based on what is available through official government APIs and authorised data sources in India.

Fully automated · Partially automated · Manual

Identity and employment checks are almost entirely API-driven now.

Identity verification (Aadhaar, PAN). Fully automated. Aadhaar verification is available through UIDAI-authorised AUAs and ASAs, and PAN verification through NSDL or Protean. Both return results in under a minute. These are the fastest and cheapest checks to run via API. [citation:14]

Employment history (EPFO/UAN). Fully automated for the formal sector. The EPFO UAN database lets you verify employment dates, employer name, and often designation. This covers any candidate who has been on payroll with a registered employer. [citation:14]

Education verification. Partially automated. Some universities and boards have digital verification APIs or are integrated through the National Academic Depository (NAD). Others still require manual outreach to the registrar's office. Expect a mix of instant results and multi-day manual checks depending on the institution. [citation:4]

Criminal record checks. Partially automated. Court record databases are increasingly digitised and searchable via API, but coverage varies by state and court level. Police verification always requires manual processing. [citation:2]

Address verification. Mostly manual. Digital address matching against databases is possible, but confirming that a candidate actually lives at an address still requires a field agent visit in most cases.

Reference checks. Manual. No API can conduct a meaningful reference conversation. This will remain a human task.

§ 03 — How it works

How a BGV API actually works.

The flow is simpler than it sounds. Here is what happens from the moment you trigger a check to the moment you get a result.

  • Your system sends candidate details to the API endpoint, typically name, Aadhaar number or PAN, and date of birth, along with a consent reference
  • The provider validates the request and routes each check to the appropriate source: UIDAI for Aadhaar, NSDL for PAN, EPFO for employment, and so on
  • The source returns a verified or unverified result with structured data, not a scanned PDF
  • The provider aggregates results into a single response and returns it to your system
  • Your system stores the result against the candidate record and triggers the next step in your workflow, whether that is scheduling an interview or flagging for review
Authentication Most providers use OAuth 2.0 or API keys with role-based access control.
Consent handling Under the DPDP Act, consent must be captured before the API call and referenced in the request.
Data format JSON in, JSON out. Structured fields, not documents. Machine-readable results.
Error handling Most APIs return clear codes for not found, mismatch, or source unavailable, so your system knows when to retry or escalate.
§ 04 — Legal framework

The DPDP Act and BGV APIs.

Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, background verification is legal when conducted with explicit consent, purpose disclosure, and security safeguards. The API model actually makes compliance easier than manual verification, because every step is logged and auditable.

Consent · Purpose · Audit trail

API-led verification creates a provable consent trail.

Explicit consent is mandatory. A generic clause in an employment contract is no longer sufficient. Under the DPDP Act, consent must be specific, informed, and revocable. Your API call should reference a consent record that captures exactly what will be checked and why. [citation:11]

Purpose limitation applies. Data collected for hiring cannot be reused for other purposes without fresh consent. The API model naturally enforces this because each check is a distinct request with a defined purpose. [citation:11]

Audit trails are automatic. Every API call is timestamped and logged. If a candidate later challenges how their data was used, you have a provable record of what was checked, when, and under what consent. [citation:11]

Penalties for non-compliance are severe. The DPDP Act allows fines up to ₹250 crore for serious data breaches. Consent-led API workflows are the safest approach for any organisation running background checks at scale. [citation:5]

Third-party processors must be documented. Your BGV API provider acts as a data processor on your behalf. Your data processing agreement should clearly define their role, obligations, and breach notification responsibilities. [citation:5]

§ 05 — Cost and speed

Manual vs API: cost and speed.

The cost difference between manual and API-driven verification is not marginal. It is structural.

Metric Manual BGV API-driven BGV
Cost per candidate ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 ₹50 to ₹800 depending on checks
Turnaround time 5 to 15 days Under 60 seconds for digital checks
Source authentication Often indirect or unverifiable Direct from government sources
Scalability Limited by human capacity Thousands per day with no degradation
Audit trail Manual, often incomplete Automatic, timestamped, immutable

For a company hiring 500 people per year, the difference between manual and API-driven verification can exceed ₹8 lakh in annual savings, not counting the reduction in time-to-hire. [citation:18]

§ 06 — Choosing a provider

How to choose a BGV API provider.

The Indian market has several providers offering BGV APIs. Not all are equal. Here is what to evaluate before you commit to an integration.

  • Direct source authorisation. Does the provider have direct API access to UIDAI, NSDL, and EPFO, or do they resell through another aggregator? Direct access means faster results and fewer failure points.
  • DPDP compliance tooling. Do they provide consent capture, purpose tracking, and audit logs as part of the API, or do you have to build those yourself?
  • Coverage of checks. Can they handle Aadhaar, PAN, EPFO, court records, and education in one integration, or do you need multiple providers?
  • Sandbox environment. Can you test the API without real candidate data before going live?
  • Pricing transparency. Per-check pricing published on the website, or do you need to book a sales call just to see the rates?
  • Field verification fallback. When a digital check cannot complete, do they have field agents to finish the job manually?
