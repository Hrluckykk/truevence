BGV vendor RFP template: a ready-to-use structure for HR teams.

Most HR teams write a background verification RFP from scratch every two or three years, and miss something critical every time. This template gives you a proven structure with all the sections that actually determine the outcome: scope, mandatory requirements, evaluation criteria, commercial terms, and submission format. Copy it, adjust the specifics, send it.

§ 01 — Why use an RFP

Why a structured RFP changes the outcome.

An RFP is not paperwork for the sake of it. It is the difference between choosing a BGV vendor on the strength of a sales pitch and choosing one on the strength of documented capability. When you send the same questions to every vendor in the same format, you can compare answers directly. When you do not, you end up comparing different stories from different sales teams.

For background verification in India, the RFP matters even more than it does for most procurement. The vendor will hold candidate data, interact with government APIs, and produce reports that inform hiring decisions. Getting this wrong is not just a commercial problem, it is a compliance problem.

Purpose Get comparable, documented answers from every shortlisted vendor in one consistent format.
Timeline From RFP issue to signed contract: 4 to 6 weeks for a well-run process.
Vendors to invite Shortlist 3 to 5 vendors from initial screening. More than five dilutes the evaluation.
Evaluation window Give vendors 2 weeks to respond. Under a week produces rushed, thin submissions.

An RFP that asks the right questions saves you from a vendor that would have failed the answers. The cost is two weeks. The saving is the next two years.

§ 02 — The structure

RFP structure, section by section.

A well-built BGV RFP has nine sections. Each one exists for a specific reason. Here is what each section should contain and why it matters.

  • Introduction and background. Brief overview of your organisation, hiring volumes, and why you are evaluating BGV vendors now. Sets context and helps vendors tailor their response.
  • Scope of work. Exactly which verification checks you need, current volumes, expected growth, and geographic coverage requirements.
  • Mandatory requirements. The non-negotiables: DPDP compliance, UIDAI authorisation, minimum years in operation, India-registered entity. Vendors that miss these are disqualified before scoring.
  • Technical requirements. API capabilities, ATS integrations, webhook support, sandbox availability, data security posture, uptime commitments.
  • Service level requirements. Turnaround commitments per check type, escalation matrix, reporting cadence, and remedies for SLA misses.
  • Commercial requirements. Pricing format, rate card questions, invoicing cadence, and total cost of ownership considerations beyond the per-check rate.
  • Evaluation criteria. The exact weightings you will apply to score responses. Publishing this up front keeps vendors honest.
  • Submission format. How responses should be structured, what documents must be attached, and where to send them.
  • Timeline and contact. RFP issue date, response deadline, planned evaluation window, and the single contact person on your side.
    • § 03 — Scope of work

    The scope section: what to specify.

    The scope section is where most RFPs go wrong. Vendors respond to vague scopes with vague capabilities, and you end up discovering the gaps during onboarding. Be specific.

    Checks · Volumes · Geography

    The scope section is where you separate serious vendors from sales teams.

    List every check you need explicitly. Identity (Aadhaar, PAN), employment (EPFO and direct employer), education (university direct), criminal records (court and police), address (physical and digital), reference checks, and any sector-specific checks like medical fitness. If you do not list it, vendors will not price it.

    State your actual hiring volumes. Current annual volume, expected growth over 24 months, seasonal peaks. This lets vendors commit to turnaround that accounts for your real load, not a hypothetical average.

    Specify geographic coverage. Which cities you hire in, whether you need physical verification in Tier 2 or Tier 3 locations, and whether you have candidates returning from overseas education or employment. Coverage claims are easy to make and hard to verify. Ask for city-level detail.

    Note any sector-specific requirements. BFSI, healthcare, and IT services each have regulatory minimums. State them so vendors with no experience in your sector can self-select out.

    Include expected integration requirements. Which ATS or HRMS you use, whether you want API-driven flows or bulk uploads, and any reporting you need for compliance audits.

    § 04 — Mandatory requirements

    Mandatory requirements: the disqualifiers.

    These are the questions that filter out vendors before you spend time scoring them. If a vendor cannot meet every single one, they should not make it to the evaluation stage.

    Requirement What to ask Why it matters
    DPDP compliance Consent capture, purpose limitation, retention policy, breach notification process Your liability transfers to the vendor under the DPDP Act
    UIDAI authorisation AUA or ASA status, certificate reference number Running Aadhaar checks without authorisation is illegal
    Registered Indian entity CIN, GST registration, registered office address You need a legal counterparty in India for the contract
    Financial stability Years in operation, client concentration, audited revenue or last 2 years of tax filings A vendor that folds mid-contract leaves you with no capability overnight
    Data security posture ISO 27001, SOC 2, or equivalent. Encryption at rest and in transit You are trusting them with candidate PII
    Insurance Professional indemnity and cyber liability cover, with limits Needed if a breach or a false verification causes loss
    § 05 — Technical and SLA

    Technical and SLA requirements.

    These are the sections that distinguish a modern BGV vendor from one still running on spreadsheets and email. Ask specifically, because general answers hide weak capability.

    • Public API documentation available before contract signing, with sample request and response for each check type
    • Sandbox environment for integration testing without consuming real candidate data
    • Webhook support for real-time status notifications when checks complete or fail
    • ATS integrations with your specific system, or a documented integration path with timeline
    • Turnaround commitment per check type in writing, not a blended average
    • Peak load SLA defining what turnaround looks like when volume triples
    • Escalation matrix with named contacts at L1, L2, and L3
    • SLA remedies including service credits or TAT penalties for consistent misses
    • Uptime commitment with a public status page and historical uptime data
    • Business continuity plan for API downtime, source outages, or regional disruptions
    § 06 — Evaluation criteria

    Evaluation criteria and weightings.

    Publishing your evaluation weightings up front is the single highest-leverage thing you can do in an RFP. It tells vendors what you actually care about, and it protects you from accusations of arbitrary decision-making later.

    Category Weight Why this weight
    Coverage and capability 20% Can they actually run the checks you need
    Compliance and legal 25% Highest weight, because the liability is yours
    Accuracy and quality 15% A wrong report is worse than no report
    Turnaround and SLA 15% Directly affects your time-to-hire
    Pricing and commercials 10% Important but not the deciding factor
    Technology and integration 10% Determines how much manual work is eliminated
    Support and references 5% Tiebreaker between otherwise equal vendors
    § 07 — Submission format

    Submission format: how vendors should respond.

    Do not let vendors submit free-form responses. Specify the format so every response is directly comparable, and so you can score them in a spreadsheet instead of reading them like essays.

  • Section-by-section response that mirrors your RFP structure, with the requirement restated before each answer
  • Compliance matrix as a single table with columns for requirement, compliant (yes/no/partial), and comment
  • Pricing sheet in your provided template, not their own format, so rates are directly comparable
  • Case studies or references from at least two clients in your sector or of similar scale
  • Supporting documents including UIDAI authorisation, ISO certification, insurance certificates, and a sample report
  • Single point of contact for the RFP process, with name, designation, email, and phone
    • § 08 — Timeline

    A realistic RFP timeline.

    Four to six weeks is enough to run a proper BGV vendor RFP without dragging. Anything faster produces rushed responses and shallow evaluation. Anything slower loses momentum and the business case gets stale.

    Week Activity Owner
    Week 1 Draft RFP, internal alignment on scope and weightings HR and Procurement
    Week 2 Shortlist 3 to 5 vendors, issue RFP, hold Q&A call if needed Procurement
    Weeks 3 to 4 Vendors prepare responses, submit questions before the deadline Vendors
    Week 4 Responses due. Initial compliance screening Procurement
    Week 5 Score responses, schedule demos with top 2 vendors HR and Procurement
    Week 6 Reference calls, commercial negotiation, vendor selection Procurement and Legal
    § 09 — Common mistakes

    Common RFP mistakes to avoid.

    These are the mistakes we see most often when HR teams run their own BGV RFP. Each one has a simple fix.

    • Not publishing evaluation weightings. Vendors respond to what they think you want, not what you actually need. Publish the weightings.
    • Treating compliance as a yes/no question. Every vendor says they are compliant. Ask for documentation, not confirmation.
    • Skipping the sample report. Ask each vendor to share a redacted sample report. It is the fastest way to see the real quality of their output.
    • Ignoring the reference call. References on a slide are marketing. References you actually speak to are evidence.
    • Weighting price too high. Cheapest is often not cheapest once re-verification costs and report errors are counted.
    • No SLA remedies. A turnaround commitment without consequences is not a commitment, it is a wish.
    • Rushing the timeline. Two weeks to respond produces thin submissions. Four weeks produces considered ones.
