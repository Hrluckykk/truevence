BGV Reporting · Accuracy

How to evaluate BGV report accuracy: a practical guide.

Every background verification vendor claims high accuracy. Very few can show you how they measure it or what their actual error rate is. This guide walks through how to measure BGV report accuracy, how to spot-check reports for errors, and how to hold your vendor accountable when the numbers fall short.

July 2026 9 min read Truevence Editorial Team Filed under BGV Reporting
§ 01 — What accuracy means

What "accuracy" actually means in a BGV report.

Accuracy is not the same as completeness. A report that says "Education not verified" is complete if that is what happened. It is only accurate if the underlying check was performed correctly and the source was genuinely unreachable. Every finding in a BGV report has two properties: whether the check ran to completion, and whether the result matches what the authoritative source actually said.

Most vendors conflate the two. They report high "completion rates" and call it accuracy. For a proper accuracy measure, you need to define what counts as an accurate result and how you will verify it.

Definition A report is accurate when the finding matches what the authoritative source actually said.
Not the same as Not the same as "completion rate" or "match against a third-party database."
Digital target Above 98 percent for Aadhaar, PAN, and EPFO checks.
Physical target Above 95 percent for address and reference checks.

A BGV report is only as good as its source. A wrong source, faithfully transcribed, is still a wrong report.

§ 02 — How to measure

How to measure BGV report accuracy.

You do not need to re-verify every report to estimate accuracy reliably. A statistical sample of 5 to 10 percent of monthly reports, re-verified against the original source, gives you a defensible number for the entire population.

Sampling · Re-verification · Scoring

A 5 to 10 percent monthly sample gives you a statistically reliable accuracy number.

Sample size. For a monthly volume of 500 to 2,000 checks, sample 5 percent. For volumes above 2,000, 10 percent is unnecessary and 2 to 3 percent is enough. Pull the sample randomly from the full month, not just the last week.

Re-verification source. Re-run each sampled check against the authoritative source: UIDAI for Aadhaar, NSDL or Protean for PAN, EPFO for employment, the university directly for education. Do not use a third-party database, because that just repeats the original error.

Scoring rule. A check scores as accurate if the finding matches the authoritative source. It scores as inaccurate if the report says verified but the source says not found, or vice versa. Any partial match, where some fields match and some do not, scores as inaccurate for tracking purposes even if it is a minor discrepancy.

Sample audit cadence. Do this once a month for the first three months of any new vendor relationship, then move to quarterly once the vendor has demonstrated consistent accuracy.

Reporting to the vendor. Share your accuracy findings with the vendor every quarter. This is the only way to create pressure for improvement, and it is the only way to justify SLA remedies if accuracy falls below the committed level.

§ 03 — Common errors

Common BGV report errors and how to spot them.

BGV errors are rarely random. They cluster around a small number of failure modes, and once you know what to look for, they are easy to spot in a sample.

Error type How it appears in the report Root cause
Stale data Report confirms employment that ended 18 months ago Vendor using a database not refreshed recently
Name mismatch Report says not found, but the candidate has a common Indian name Fuzzy matching failing on common names
DOB mismatch Report confirms with a slightly different date of birth Transcription error from manual entry
Source not contacted Report says university not reachable, but the university is reachable Field agent did not attempt to contact, or gave up after one attempt
Wrong source used Report verifies against a third-party aggregator, not the university Vendor cutting corners to save time
Transcription error Report contains data that exists in the source, but is entered wrongly Manual re-typing without verification
§ 04 — Red flags

Red flags to look for in BGV report design.

Some accuracy problems are visible just from the report format, before you even re-verify a single check. If you see three or more of these in a vendor's sample report, ask for their accuracy measurement methodology before proceeding.

  • No source citation. A report that says "employment verified" without naming the source cannot be audited.
  • No timestamps. A report without the date and time of verification cannot be checked for freshness.
  • No analyst name or ID. Reports that do not name the person who completed the check have no accountability.
  • No confidence level. Some checks return partial matches. The report should say so, not present them as full verifications.
  • Blended summary at the top. A report that summarises "candidate cleared" without breaking down each check hides which one failed.
  • No distinction between verified and not found. These are different outcomes with different implications. The report should not conflate them.
  • No remarks field. Human judgment calls happen. If there is nowhere to record them, the vendor is over-simplifying.
§ 05 — Auditing your vendor

Auditing your BGV vendor's accuracy.

Once you have a measurement process in place, the next step is to build it into your regular vendor management cadence. Here is what a serious accuracy audit looks like.

  • Monthly internal sample audit of 5 to 10 percent of reports, re-verified against authoritative sources
  • Quarterly accuracy report from the vendor with their own numbers, side by side with yours
  • Root cause analysis required from the vendor if your audit and their numbers diverge by more than 2 percentage points
  • Written remediation plan if the audited accuracy falls below the SLA threshold for two consecutive months
  • Free re-verification on any check your audit finds to be inaccurate, with a defined turnaround
  • Escalation path to vendor leadership if accuracy issues persist across quarters
  • Documentation retention of your audit findings for at least 24 months, for compliance and legal purposes
    • § 06 — By check type

    Accuracy expectations by check type.

    Different checks have different achievable accuracy levels. Expecting the same number from a digital Aadhaar check and a physical address visit is unrealistic. Here are the benchmarks to hold your vendor to.

    Check type Target accuracy Notes
    Aadhaar Above 99 percent Deterministic; failures usually mean UIDAI downtime
    PAN Above 99 percent Deterministic; failures usually mean NSDL or Protean downtime
    EPFO employment Above 98 percent Depends on the employer having filed EPFO returns
    Education Above 95 percent Depends on the university's response rate
    Criminal record Above 97 percent District-level court coverage varies
    Address (physical) Above 95 percent Depends on field agent quality and address accuracy
    Reference checks Above 90 percent Referees are unresponsive a significant fraction of the time

    If a vendor cannot meet these targets, the conversation is either about their process or about their honesty. Both are worth having before you scale volume with them.

    § 07 — Remediation

    What to do when accuracy drops.

    Every vendor will have bad months. The question is what happens next. A properly managed vendor relationship has a defined escalation and remediation path for accuracy issues.

  • First occurrence: raise the issue in the monthly operational review, request root cause analysis within 10 business days
  • Second consecutive month: escalate to vendor operations leadership, request a written remediation plan with timelines
  • Third consecutive month: invoke SLA remedies, which should include service credits or free re-verification of affected checks
  • Beyond three months: escalate to vendor executive leadership with a formal notice of breach
  • Persistent failure: exercise the termination right defined in the SLA if accuracy does not return above threshold within a defined window
