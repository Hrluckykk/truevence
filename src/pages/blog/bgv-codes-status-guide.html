The BGV marking system explained: what Green, Amber, and Red codes really mean.

If you have just received a background verification report and you are staring at colour codes and short status tags, this guide is for you. We break down the BGV marking system so you can read every background verification report code with confidence before you make a hiring decision.

July 2026 8 min read Truevence Editorial Team Filed under BGV Reporting
§ 01 — What it is

What is the BGV marking system?

The BGV marking system is the standardised way background verification companies tag the outcome of every check they run. Instead of handing you a long raw report and letting you interpret it, vendors condense each verification result into a short code or colour, so an HR team can scan a report in seconds and know what to do next.

Most BGV providers in India use a colour-coded scheme (Green, Amber, Red) alongside short status codes like Clear, Minor Discrepancy, Adverse. Some vendors only use letters and numbers, but the underlying logic is the same: did the information check out, does it need review, or did it fail?

Purpose Turn complex verification data into one clear, consistent signal.
Common formats Colour codes (Green, Amber, Red) and letter codes like Clear, Discrepancy, Adverse.
Why it matters Enables fair, consistent hiring decisions across your entire pipeline.
Who uses it HR teams, staffing agencies, BFSI compliance teams, and gig platforms that onboard at scale.

A BGV code is not a verdict. It's a signal, and how you respond to it defines the quality of your hiring.

§ 02 — The three signals

The colour code system in a BGV report.

Most BGV vendors in India use a three-colour scheme. Each colour tells you how the check landed and what action it calls for.

✓

Green means Clear and Verified

All details matched · No action needed

All details matched official records and no discrepancies were found. The candidate is cleared for onboarding. This is the ideal outcome, and it means you can proceed without further review.

!

Amber (or Yellow) means Partial Discrepancy

Minor mismatch · Review with candidate

A small inconsistency was found, like a date mismatch or a spelling variation. These cases need review with the candidate or hiring manager before you decide. Amber is a prompt, not a rejection.

✗

Red means Adverse or Not Verified

Major discrepancy · Escalate to HR

A major discrepancy or fraud was detected, such as fake documents, a hidden criminal record, or a misrepresented employment gap. These cases should be escalated to HR leadership before any hiring decision.

§ 03 — Common status codes

BGV status codes and what they mean.

Beyond colours, most reports use a short status code. Here are the ones you will see most often, along with the action each one calls for.

Code Meaning Action
CLEAR / GREEN All information verified, no mismatch Proceed with hiring
PENDING Verification is still in progress Await final report
INCONCLUSIVE Unable to verify due to lack of response or data Request additional documents
MINOR DISCREPANCY Small mismatch in dates, spelling, etc. Review with candidate
ADVERSE / RED Fake documents, criminal history, or misrepresented employment gap Escalate to HR leadership
UNABLE TO VERIFY Institution or employer not reachable Attempt alternate verification
§ 04 — Codes by check type

Codes specific to each verification type.

Some BGV reports also include check-type-specific codes. These give you granular insight into which specific document or source failed, and why.

Identity · Education · Criminal

Granular codes that tell you exactly what happened.

Identity check codes. ID-VERIFIED means the Aadhaar or PAN was confirmed via UIDAI or NSDL. ID-MISMATCH means the name or DOB did not match official records. ID-FAKE means the document was found to be forged.

Education check codes. EDU-VERIFIED means the degree was confirmed directly with the university. EDU-NOTFOUND means the university has no record of the candidate. EDU-INCOMPLETE means the course was not completed.

Criminal check codes. CRIM-CLEAR means no adverse record was found. CRIM-FLAGGED means a possible match was found and is under review. CRIM-CONFIRMED means the criminal record was confirmed.

§ 05 — Hiring workflow

How to use BGV codes in your hiring workflow.

Establish an internal policy that clearly defines which status codes automatically clear a candidate, which require review, and which lead to rejection. This ensures fairness and consistency across your entire team.

  • Auto-clear all Green reports for faster onboarding
  • Review Amber cases with the candidate and hiring manager
  • Escalate Red cases to a committee for a final decision
  • Track Pending and Inconclusive cases separately, and never treat them as adverse
§ 06 — Why it matters

Why standardised BGV codes matter.

Using clear, consistent verification codes eliminates confusion, reduces bias, and helps you scale your hiring without second-guessing every report. It also makes vendor transitions easier, since you can map old vendor codes to new ones without rewriting your internal policies.

For compliance-heavy sectors like BFSI, healthcare, and IT, a standardised marking system is also a defensive measure. If a hiring decision is ever questioned, a documented code and a clear response policy make it easy to show that the decision was consistent, fair, and based on evidence.

§ 07 — Frequently asked

Frequently asked questions about BGV marking.

What is the BGV marking system? +
The BGV marking system is a standardised way background verification companies tag the outcome of each check. Most vendors use a three-colour scheme (Green, Amber, Red) or short status codes like Clear, Minor Discrepancy, and Adverse to indicate whether a candidate's information was verified, needs review, or was found to be false.
What does Green mean in a BGV report? +
Green means the check was Clear and Verified. All details matched official records, no discrepancies were found, and the candidate is cleared for onboarding.
What does Amber or Yellow mean in a BGV report? +
Amber or Yellow means a Partial Discrepancy was found. This is usually a minor mismatch such as a date difference or a spelling variation. The case needs review with the candidate or hiring manager before a decision is made. Amber is a prompt, not a rejection.
What does Red mean in a BGV report? +
Red means Adverse or Not Verified. A major discrepancy or fraud was detected, such as fake documents, a hidden criminal record, or a misrepresented employment gap. Red cases should be escalated to HR leadership before any hiring decision.
What is the difference between Pending and Inconclusive in BGV? +
Pending means the verification is still in progress and the final report is not yet ready. Inconclusive means the vendor attempted the check but could not verify the information due to a lack of response, missing data, or an unreachable source. Neither should be treated as adverse.
Can a vendor use different codes from another vendor? +
Yes. Terminology varies slightly between vendors. Some use colour-based signals, others use short letter codes. What matters is that the underlying meaning is consistent: Clear, Needs Review, or Adverse. When switching vendors, map each old code to its nearest equivalent in the new system so your internal policies stay intact.
