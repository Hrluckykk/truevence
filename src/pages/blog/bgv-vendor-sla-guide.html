Vendor Management · SLA Guide

BGV vendor SLA: what employers should actually expect.

Every BGV vendor promises fast turnaround. Very few put it in writing with remedies attached. This guide explains exactly what a background verification service level agreement should contain, which numbers to hold vendors to, and which SLA terms are meaningless without consequences.

July 2026 9 min read Truevence Editorial Team Filed under Vendor Management
§ 01 — What an SLA is

What a BGV vendor SLA actually is.

A service level agreement is the part of your contract with a background verification vendor that defines what counts as acceptable performance, how it will be measured, and what happens when the vendor falls short. Without an SLA, "we will complete most checks in 3 to 5 business days" is a marketing claim. With an SLA, it is a contractual commitment with a defined remedy if missed.

Most BGV vendors have a standard SLA they will share on request. Some of them are genuinely rigorous. Many are vague, unmeasurable, or missing remedies. The evaluation question is not whether the vendor has an SLA, but whether the SLA actually protects your hiring process.

Purpose Turn vendor performance commitments into measurable, enforceable obligations.
Covers Turnaround, accuracy, availability, support responsiveness, and escalation.
Enforcement Service credits, escalation to leadership, or termination rights for repeated misses.
Review cadence Monthly operational review, quarterly business review with performance data.

An SLA without remedies is not a commitment. It is a wish with a deadline attached.

§ 02 — Turnaround

Turnaround: the numbers to hold a vendor to.

Turnaround is the SLA term most HR teams care about, and the one most vendors try to obscure behind a blended average. A single "2 to 5 business days" commitment hides which check is actually slow. A good SLA breaks turnaround down by check type.

Check type Standard SLA Premium SLA
Identity (Aadhaar, PAN) Within 24 hours Within 4 working hours
Employment (EPFO) Within 3 business days Within 24 hours
Education (university direct) 5 to 7 business days 3 to 5 business days
Criminal record check 3 to 5 business days Within 48 hours
Address (physical) 5 to 7 business days 3 to 5 business days
Reference check Within 5 business days Within 3 business days

These numbers reflect what a well-operated BGV vendor in India can consistently deliver in 2026. If a vendor is quoting materially longer, either their internal operations are manual or they are sandbagging. If they are quoting materially shorter, ask for evidence from their last 100 checks before believing the claim.

§ 03 — Accuracy

Accuracy: the commitment most SLAs leave out.

Turnaround gets all the attention, but accuracy is where a bad BGV vendor does the most damage. A wrong employment verification can cost a candidate a job they deserved. A missed criminal record can cost the company far more than that. The SLA should define accuracy and commit to a number.

Definition · Rate · Remedy

Accuracy is meaningless without a definition and a remedy.

Define what counts as accurate. An accurate result means the report matches what the source actually said, and the source is the authoritative one for that check. Not a database that might be out of date, not a partial match. The SLA should state this clearly.

Commit to a rate above 98 percent for digital checks. Aadhaar, PAN, and EPFO checks are deterministic. Any vendor claiming less than 98 percent accuracy on these is either measuring wrong or operating poorly.

Commit to a rate above 95 percent for physical checks. Address and reference checks involve human judgment and are inherently less deterministic. 95 percent is a reasonable floor.

Free re-verification on challenge. If the employer can demonstrate a finding was inaccurate, the vendor should re-run the check at no cost. This should be explicit in the SLA, with a defined timeline for the re-verification.

Root cause reporting. If more than 2 percent of checks in a month are inaccurate, the vendor should provide a written root cause analysis within 10 business days. This is what separates serious vendors from those that just say sorry.

§ 04 — Availability and support

Availability and support commitments.

For any BGV vendor with an API or a candidate-facing portal, uptime matters. And support responsiveness matters even when the system is up, because complex cases do happen.

  • API uptime commitment of at least 99.5 percent monthly, with a public status page showing historical uptime
  • Scheduled maintenance windows announced at least 48 hours in advance, and outside your business hours
  • First response time for support tickets within 4 business hours, for any issue
  • Resolution time for critical issues within 1 business day, or escalation to senior leadership
  • Named account manager available during business hours, with a mobile number for urgent issues
  • Escalation path documented from account manager through to leadership, with names and contact details
  • Quarterly business review scheduled as part of the engagement, with performance data presented
§ 05 — Remedies

Remedies: what happens when the SLA is missed.

This is the section that makes the rest of the SLA meaningful. A turnaround commitment without a remedy is not a commitment. A remedy without teeth is not a remedy. The three tiers below represent what a properly negotiated BGV SLA should contain.

Miss type Remedy Escalation
Occasional TAT miss Written explanation within 3 business days Account manager
Recurring TAT miss Service credit of 5 to 10 percent on the affected invoices Operations leadership
Accuracy breach Free re-verification plus root cause report Quality leadership
Sustained SLA failure Right to terminate without penalty within 30 days Executive leadership
Data breach Notification within 24 hours, full incident report within 72 hours Immediate, all levels

The specific numbers in the service credit column are negotiable. What is not negotiable is that there are numbers there in the first place. A vendor confident in their operations will accept them. A vendor confident only in their sales pitch will resist.

§ 06 — DPDP and breach SLAs

Data breach and DPDP SLAs.

Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, when your BGV vendor processes candidate data on your behalf, the compliance obligation still sits with you as the data fiduciary. The SLA is where you lock in the vendor's breach notification obligations so you can meet your own regulatory timelines.

  • Breach notification within 24 hours of the vendor becoming aware of a suspected incident
  • Full incident report within 72 hours including scope, root cause, and remediation steps
  • Cooperation with your regulator notification if the breach requires reporting to the Data Protection Board
  • Data processing agreement referenced in the SLA, with retention and deletion obligations
  • Security audit rights so you can verify the vendor's posture annually or after any incident
  • Liability allocation clearly stated for breaches originating on the vendor side
§ 07 — Common gaps

Common BGV SLA gaps to watch for.

These are the SLA weaknesses we see most often when HR teams share their vendor contracts with us. Each one is fixable at the negotiation stage, and much harder to fix once the contract is signed.

  • Blended turnaround numbers. If the SLA says "average 3 business days" without breaking it down by check type, one slow check can hide behind five fast ones.
  • No peak-load language. Turnaround commitments that only apply at normal volumes are meaningless during your actual peak hiring months.
  • Exclusion for "source delays". Every vendor will want to exclude delays caused by government sources. This is reasonable in principle, but should be capped, not unlimited.
  • Vague accuracy definitions. Without a stated definition and a rate, "high accuracy" is just an adjective.
  • No remedy for the second miss. The first miss can be forgiven. The second miss needs a consequence, not another apology.
  • Undefined escalation path. A named contact who is not actually available is worse than no contact at all.
  • Breach notification with no timeline. "We will notify as soon as possible" is not a timeline.
