Can an employee challenge a BGV report?

Yes. Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, employees have the right to access their background verification report, request correction of inaccurate data, and dispute findings that are wrong. Here is exactly how the challenge process works, what you can dispute, and what employers are required to do.

July 2026 9 min read Truevence Editorial Team Filed under Candidate Rights
§ 01 — The short answer

Yes — and here is why you can.

In India, employees and candidates have a legal right to challenge an incorrect background verification report. This right flows from the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDP Act), which gives data principals — that is, the individuals whose data is being processed — specific rights over their personal information.

Before the DPDP Act, challenging a BGV report was largely a matter of the employer's goodwill. There was no statutory framework. Today, it is a legal right, and employers who ignore disputes can face regulatory consequences. [citation:17]

Your right Access your BGV report · Correct inaccurate data · Dispute wrong findings.
Legal basis DPDP Act 2023, which grants data principals rights over personal data used in verification. [citation:17]
Employer duty Employers and BGV vendors must provide a correction mechanism and respond to disputes.
Timeline Most employers allow 7 to 15 days from report delivery to raise a dispute. Check your policy.

A BGV report is not a final verdict. It is a set of findings that you have a legal right to question and correct.

§ 02 — What you can challenge

What you can dispute in a BGV report.

Not every finding is disputable. You cannot dispute a true adverse record simply because you do not like it. But you can dispute anything that is factually inaccurate, incorrectly attributed, or based on a data error.

1.

Incorrect personal data.

Wrong name spelling, incorrect date of birth, wrong address, mismatched ID number. These are data errors, not findings, and they must be corrected. This is the most common and most fixable type of dispute.

Name errors DOB mismatch Address wrong
2.

Misattributed records.

A criminal record that belongs to someone else with a similar name. A court case that was withdrawn or acquitted but shows as pending. An employment record attributed to you but actually belonging to another person. Identity confusion is a real problem in Indian BGV.

Same-name confusion Closed cases shown open Wrong person's record
3.

Outdated or incomplete data.

A degree that was awarded but the university database was not updated. An employment period that is correct but the EPFO record is incomplete. A court case that was disposed of but the database still shows it as active. The source data is wrong, not you.

Database stale EPFO gaps Court record not updated
4.

Process errors by the verifier.

The verifier contacted the wrong university, queried the wrong court jurisdiction, mistyped an ID number, or failed to follow up when the source did not respond. These are procedural failures, not findings about you.

Wrong source Typo in query No follow-up
§ 03 — What you cannot dispute

What is not disputable.

Not everything can be challenged. If the finding is accurate, the dispute will not succeed — and raising a dispute on a true finding can damage your credibility.

  • A criminal record that is genuinely yours. If you have a conviction and it was correctly found, you cannot dispute the finding itself. You can only dispute errors in how it is described or classified.
  • An employment gap you did not disclose. If the EPFO record shows a period of employment you omitted, the finding is accurate. The dispute is about your disclosure, not the record.
  • A degree you did not complete. If you claimed a degree and the university confirms you did not graduate, the finding is correct.
  • Your own prior statements. If you provided information in your application that contradicts what the verifier found, you cannot dispute the verifier's finding — you can only explain the discrepancy.

The distinction matters. A dispute is a correction mechanism for errors, not an appeal against accurate findings. Use it for the former, not the latter.

§ 04 — How to challenge

How to challenge a BGV report: step by step.

Here is the process you should follow if you believe your BGV report contains an error.

Step What to do Who to contact
1. Request the report Ask for a copy of the BGV report from your employer or the vendor HR / BGV vendor
2. Identify the error Pinpoint exactly which check is wrong and why Yourself
3. Gather evidence Collect documents proving the correct information (certificates, letters, ID) Yourself
4. Submit dispute Write a formal dispute with evidence attached HR / Vendor grievance officer
5. Await response Most disputes are acknowledged within 3-5 days HR / Vendor
6. Re-verification If accepted, the check is re-run with corrected data BGV vendor
7. Corrected report The report is updated and shared with the employer HR / Vendor
§ 05 — Your legal rights

Your rights under the DPDP Act.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 gives you specific, enforceable rights over your personal data — including data used in background verification. [citation:17]

Access · Correction · Erasure · Grievance

The DPDP Act makes dispute resolution a legal right, not a favour.

Right to access. You have the right to know what personal data is being processed about you, for what purpose, and to whom it has been disclosed. This includes the BGV report itself. [citation:17]

Right to correction. If your personal data is inaccurate, incomplete, or outdated, you have the right to request correction. The data fiduciary — your employer or the BGV vendor — must act on a valid correction request. [citation:17]

Right to erasure. When the purpose for which your data was collected is no longer being served, you have the right to request erasure, subject to legal retention requirements. [citation:17]

Right to grievance redressal. Every data fiduciary must provide a mechanism for you to raise grievances. If your dispute is not resolved, you can escalate to the Data Protection Board of India. [citation:17]

Employer obligations. Employers must obtain explicit, informed, and revocable consent before conducting BGV. They must also ensure that any BGV vendor they use complies with the same standards. [citation:17]

§ 06 — For employers

What employers must do when a candidate disputes a report.

If you are an employer, a BGV dispute is not an inconvenience. It is a legal obligation. Here is what you must do.

  • Provide the report. Candidates have a right to see the report. Do not withhold it. Share it in full, with the checks that were run and the findings recorded.
  • Acknowledge the dispute promptly. Acknowledge receipt within 3-5 business days. Silence creates legal risk and erodes trust.
  • Investigate fairly. Do not assume the candidate is wrong. Data errors are common. Look at the evidence and the source.
  • Re-verify if warranted. If the candidate provides evidence that the finding is wrong, instruct the BGV vendor to re-run the check with corrected data.
  • Correct the report. If the dispute is valid, the report must be corrected and the employer's records updated. Any adverse action based on the wrong finding should be reversed or reconsidered.
  • Document the process. Keep a record of the dispute, the evidence, the investigation, and the outcome. This is your compliance record under the DPDP Act.
  • Never retaliate. Disputing a BGV report is a legal right. Penalising a candidate for exercising it is a violation.
