BGV vendor evaluation checklist: 47 points to score every provider.

Most HR teams compare background verification vendors on price and turnaround, then sign a contract, then discover the gaps three months in. This checklist gives you 47 concrete points to evaluate before you commit, organised into eight categories. Score each provider side by side and the decision becomes obvious.

July 2026 11 min read Truevence Editorial Team Filed under Vendor Management
§ 01 — How to use this

How to use this checklist.

This checklist is designed to be scored, not just read. For each of the 47 points, give every vendor you are evaluating a score of 0 (missing), 1 (partial), or 2 (fully met). Add the scores at the end and compare. Anything below 60 out of 94 is a red flag, regardless of how attractive the pricing looks.

Not every point matters equally for every organisation. A startup hiring 20 people a year will weight pricing and turnaround higher. An enterprise in BFSI will weight compliance and accuracy higher. Adjust the weightings to reflect your reality, but do not skip any category entirely. Every category below has a failure mode that has cost real companies real money.

Scoring 0 = missing, 1 = partial, 2 = fully met. Total possible: 94 across 47 points.
Green threshold Above 75 is a strong vendor. Between 60 and 75 needs specific justification.
Red threshold Below 60 is a red flag. Walk away, or negotiate hard.
Weighting Adjust category weights for your hiring volume and sector before you start scoring vendors.

The wrong BGV vendor does not fail loudly. It quietly lets bad hires through, and you find out months later.

§ 02 — Coverage and capability

Coverage and capability (8 points).

Can the vendor actually run every check you need, in every location you need it?

  • Identity verification available via UIDAI and NSDL direct authorisation
  • Employment verification through EPFO and direct employer outreach, not just third-party databases
  • Education verification with direct university access, including regional universities outside major metros
  • Criminal record checks across district courts, high courts, and where relevant, police records
  • Address verification with field agents in every city you hire in, not partner arrangements
  • Reference checks conducted by trained interviewers, not automated forms sent to referees
  • Pan-India reach covering at least 25 states with in-house capacity, not subcontractors
  • Global checks for international hires or overseas education, if relevant to your hiring
§ 03 — Compliance and legal

Compliance and legal (7 points).

This is the category most HR teams under-evaluate, and the one that creates the largest liability. Under the DPDP Act, your BGV vendor is a data processor and their failures become your failures.

DPDP · UIDAI · Contractual clarity

A vendor without DPDP tooling is a compliance risk you inherit.

Explicit candidate consent capture. Does the vendor provide consent capture that meets the specific, informed, and revocable standard under the DPDP Act, or does your team have to build it separately?

Purpose limitation enforcement. Can the vendor demonstrate that data collected for hiring is not reused for other purposes, and can they prove it on request?

Full audit trail. Every check should be timestamped with the consent record referenced. If a candidate challenges how their data was used, the vendor should be able to produce a complete trail within 24 hours.

UIDAI AUA or ASA status. If they run Aadhaar checks, they must be an authorised requesting entity. Ask for the certification.

Data retention policy aligned with your own. The vendor's default retention period should match your legal retention requirement, not exceed it.

Data processing agreement signed before any candidate data flows, with clear breach notification timelines.

Penalty exposure disclosed. Under the DPDP Act, serious breaches carry penalties up to ₹250 crore. Confirm in writing who carries that liability if the breach originates on the vendor side.

§ 04 — Accuracy and quality

Accuracy and quality (6 points).

A vendor that produces clean reports is worth more than a vendor that produces fast reports. Speed without accuracy is worse than no check at all.

  • Documented accuracy rate with a source, not a marketing claim
  • Multi-layer quality review before any report leaves the vendor
  • Source citation in every report so you can verify a specific finding if challenged
  • False positive policy that defines what happens when a check returns an inconclusive result
  • Re-verification process when a candidate disputes a finding, with clear timelines and no additional charge
  • Sample report shared during evaluation, so you can see the actual output before you commit
§ 05 — Turnaround and SLAs

Turnaround and service levels (5 points).

Every vendor will claim fast turnaround. The evaluation question is whether they will put it in writing, with consequences.

Point What to check Red flag
TAT by check type Documented turnaround per check, not a single blended number Only says "2 to 5 days" without breaking it down
Peak load commitment Written commitment on TAT when volume spikes 3x "We will manage it" with no specifics
Escalation matrix Named contacts for L1, L2, and L3 escalation Only a generic support email
SLA remedies Service credits or TAT penalties written into the contract No consequences for missing SLA
Status visibility Real-time dashboard or API status for every open check Only email updates when a check completes
§ 06 — Pricing and commercials

Pricing and commercials (6 points).

Pricing is not just the rate card. It is the entire commercial structure, and hidden costs are the most common source of frustration six months into a contract.

  • Published per-check pricing rather than "contact sales" for every quote
  • No retainer or minimum volume unless your hiring is genuinely predictable
  • Bulk discount tiers disclosed upfront, not negotiated privately
  • No hidden fees for re-verification, field visits, or report re-issue
  • Invoicing cadence that matches your finance cycle (monthly, not quarterly prepay)
  • Rate lock period so pricing does not jump mid-year without notice
§ 07 — Technology and integration

Technology and integration (7 points).

If the vendor cannot plug into your existing systems, every check becomes a manual copy-paste exercise. The evaluation should test integration seriously, not just accept a promise.

  • Public API documentation available before signing, not just after
  • Sandbox environment for testing without using real candidate data
  • ATS integrations with at least two of the systems you use (Workday, Darwinbox, Zoho Recruit, etc.)
  • Bulk upload support for cases where API is not possible, with the same turnaround
  • Webhook notifications so your system is notified when a check completes
  • Report export in formats your compliance team can archive (PDF with audit metadata)
  • Uptime commitment with a documented SLA and status page, not just verbal assurance
§ 08 — Support and account management

Support and account management (4 points).

Every vendor is responsive before you sign. The question is whether they are responsive at month six, when you have a complex case and need someone to pick up the phone.

  • Named account manager assigned to your organisation, not a shared support queue
  • Business hours coverage in your working timezone, with after-hours support for critical cases
  • Quarterly business review scheduled as part of the engagement, not offered as an upsell
  • Documented escalation path from account manager up to leadership, so issues do not get stuck at L1
§ 09 — Reference and stability

Reference and stability (4 points).

The final category tests the vendor's track record and financial stability. A vendor that disappears mid-contract leaves you with no verification capability overnight.

  • Referenceable customers in your sector who will take a call, not just names on a slide
  • Years in operation with continuity of leadership, not a two-year-old shell company
  • Financial stability signals: registered entity, GST compliant, not dependent on a single client
  • Business continuity plan documented for service interruptions, including alternate verification routes
§ 10 — Reading the score

Reading the final score.

Once you have scored all 47 points, sum them. The interpretation is straightforward.

Score Interpretation Action
80 to 94 Strong vendor, clear fit for your needs Proceed to contract negotiation
65 to 79 Solid vendor with specific gaps Negotiate the gaps before signing
50 to 64 Significant gaps in critical categories Reconsider unless pricing is exceptional
Below 50 Fundamental capability or compliance issues Walk away

Pay particular attention to the compliance category. A vendor that scores well on everything else but fails compliance is not a viable partner in 2026. Under the DPDP Act, that risk transfers to you the moment data starts flowing.
