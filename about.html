WHY WE STARTED

Background verification should feel like a conversation, not a transaction.

We founded Truevence because we saw HR teams stuck between slow, impersonal vendors and the fear of a bad hire. So we built a company where every client gets a direct line to the people doing the work. No call centres. No ticket queues. Just a founder‑led team that treats your candidate's background as if it were our own hire.

HOW WE WORK

Four principles that guide every verification we run.

Confidentiality First

Candidate data never leaves our secure environment. We share only the final report, never the raw documents, unless a specific check requires it.

Consent‑Led Process

We never start a check without documented candidate consent. It's the law, and it's also the right thing to do.

Responsive Turnaround

You get a dedicated point of contact who updates you without you having to ask. We're small enough that no case gets lost.

Founder‑Led Oversight

Every process is designed and reviewed by the founders. When you email, you reach the people who built the system.

OUR PROMISE

We're honest about what we can – and can't – do.

As a growing startup, we don't pretend to have hundreds of employees or decades of history. Here's what you can absolutely count on:

Accuracy over speed

We won't rush a report just to meet an artificial deadline. A wrong "clear" is worse than a delayed flag.

Full transparency

If a check is taking longer because a university is slow to respond, we tell you. No hiding behind "in progress."

Data you can delete

We retain candidate data only as long as you need it. After that, we purge it. Period.

No lock‑in contracts

We earn your business every month. You can stop anytime – no penalty, no awkward call.

OUR CULTURE OF PRECISION

We are obsessed with getting the small things right.

A single digit wrong in a date, a misspelt name, an unchecked court jurisdiction – these are not minor errors when someone's career is at stake. Our team follows a simple internal mantra: “Verify every fact as if the candidate were your next colleague.” This means cross‑referencing, calling twice when one answer seems off, and never settling for a “maybe.”
